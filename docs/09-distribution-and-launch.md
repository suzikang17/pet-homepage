# Distribution and Launch

## The honest reality

Distribution is the thing that kills most consumer products, not technology. Building the product is the fun part; grinding distribution for 6-18 months is where the real work lives. Budget for this mentally.

**Distribution is also the actual moat.** See [[14-wedge-and-moat]] for the full argument, but the short version: Homepage has a product wedge (email ingestion) that gives a 6-18 month head start, but no classic product moat. The real defensibility comes from shareable URLs, SEO compounding, and the accumulated memory data that makes users emotionally attached to their pet's page. The founder who wins is the one who treats distribution as 50% of the work, not an afterthought.

## Target user recap

See [[01-problem-and-users]]. Primary persona: 28-45 year old pet parents who already treat their pet like family and spend willingly on pet stuff.

## Channels to try (ranked by feasibility for a lean solo effort)

### 1. Pet subreddits (free, medium effort)

- **r/dogs** — large general audience
- **r/puppy101** — new owners are the ideal early users, high willingness to try tools
- **r/puppies** — similar
- **r/cats** — for cat-owner segment
- **r/dogadvice** — real pain points, users seeking solutions
- **r/dogcare** — same
- **r/dogowners**

Approach: don't spam. Contribute genuinely to conversations. When a relevant "how do you organize vet records?" or "how do you remember all the vet appointments?" thread comes up, mention the tool as a helpful resource, not a promotion. Reddit still works if you're not slimy.

### 2. Instagram / TikTok pet content (medium-high effort, potentially huge upside)

- Pet-content Instagram is massive and growing
- Make content featuring *your own pet* that happens to show the page
- The Greece trip page aesthetic demonstrates you can design things worth sharing
- Year-in-review posts are naturally shareable content
- Memory cards from the product could become user-generated Instagram posts

### 3. Vet office partnerships (low effort, medium reach)

- Hand out printed cards in local vet offices offering a free year to new clients
- Partner with small independent clinics before trying to approach big chains
- See [[11-vet-partnerships]] for full strategy

### 4. SEO long-tail (slow, high-leverage)

Search terms that are real and unsaturated:

- "how to organize dog vet records"
- "pet medical records app"
- "keep track of pet vaccinations"
- "remember pet preventative reminders"
- "share pet info with pet sitter"
- "pet memory book"
- "pet vet visit prep"

Publishing genuinely useful content targeting these queries compounds over months and years. Not a short-term channel.

### 5. Pet influencer partnerships (medium effort, variable results)

Target the 5k-50k follower tier, not the huge accounts. They're cheaper, more authentic, and more likely to actually use the product rather than just posting a sponsored photo. Cat accounts especially underserved by other pet tech.

### 6. Product Hunt launch (single-day burst, requires preparation)

A good PH launch day can bring 500-1000 signups in 24 hours. Requires a polished landing page, a clear hero message, and ideally a small existing community to upvote early. Aim for a launch once the product is genuinely ready to handle traffic and deliver on its promise.

### 7. Your own network (free, small but high quality)

Start with friends who have pets. Ask for honest feedback. Ask them to share if they love it. Word of mouth from real users is the highest quality signal you'll get.

### 8. Paid acquisition (expensive, experimental)

- Google Ads on specific long-tail keywords
- Instagram ads to pet-owner demographics
- Small test budgets ($200-500) to see if the conversion economics work
- Do this only after you've found some organic signal to amplify

## Content strategy

The pet space rewards helpful, genuine content. Blog posts that target the SEO terms above should be:

- Actually useful (not thin content with affiliate links)
- Specific to real scenarios (not generic "how to take care of a dog")
- Show the product in context naturally
- Written by someone who clearly has a pet and has lived these problems

Example posts:
- "How I finally got my dog's vaccine records organized"
- "The vet visit prep I wish I'd had 5 years ago"
- "What to tell a pet sitter: the checklist I use for my dog"
- "Why your vet bills keep getting lost and what to do about it"

## Launch sequencing

### Pre-launch (weeks before shipping)

- Build the product to shipping quality
- Set up a minimal landing page with an email waitlist
- Start posting on personal Instagram/X about building it (story-based, not promotional)
- Write 3-5 foundational blog posts for SEO
- Prepare Product Hunt assets (logo, hero video, screenshots)

### Soft launch (week 1)

- Ship to your network (5-10 pet-owning friends)
- Collect feedback, iterate
- Don't promote publicly yet

### Friends + friends of friends (week 2-4)

- Expand to ~30-50 users through personal network
- Watch retention and engagement closely
- Keep iterating

### Public launch (month 2-3)

- Product Hunt launch day
- Coordinated posts on target subreddits
- Reach out to 10-20 small pet influencers
- Monitor for signs of organic traction

### Grinding phase (month 3-12)

- SEO content cadence (1-2 posts/week)
- Ongoing subreddit participation
- Iterative product improvements from user feedback
- Double down on whatever channel is converting

## The distribution mindset

You have to fall in love with the grind, or find someone who does. Product work is fun; distribution work is uncomfortable. Most founders avoid it and wonder why their product dies. Budget mental energy for this, not just technical energy.

## Metrics that matter early

- Signups per day
- % of signups that forward their first email (activation)
- % of signups that open the monthly digest
- % of signups still active after 30 days
- % of signups that share the page with someone else
- % of signups that convert to paid (much later)
