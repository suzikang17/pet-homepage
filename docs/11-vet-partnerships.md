# Vet Partnerships

## TL;DR

Vet practices are a real long-term distribution channel, but not a first-year priority. **PetDesk already owns the "app your vet recommends" shelf.** You can't win by attacking their strength (clinic workflow integration); you win by being the app pet owners actually love using on their own, then leveraging that love into vet referrals later.

## The landscape (see also [[06-competition]])

### Vets' existing tools

- **PetDesk** — dominant player in client-facing apps. Free for pet owners, paid by clinics. Syncs with veterinary practice management software (PIMS). Handles reminders and appointment booking.
- **VitusVet** — similar positioning.
- **Rapport / PetPro Connect / Allydvm** — backend client communication tools.
- **Covetrus, IDEXX, Cornerstone, AVImark** — practice management software (the core business systems).

### Why vets adopt tools

Vet practices care about (roughly in order):

1. **Reducing no-shows** for appointments — biggest pain point in practice economics
2. **Client retention** — getting clients back for annual wellness, boosters, dentals
3. **Pre-appointment information** — knowing what's up before the visit saves time
4. **Compliance on post-visit care** — did they give the meds? did they come back for the recheck?
5. **Differentiation** — premium clinics want something nice to offer that other clinics don't
6. **Client acquisition / marketing** — weaker but not zero

### What vets DO NOT want

- Another piece of software to manage
- Integration work with their PIMS
- Liability for client data they don't control
- Another vendor relationship to maintain
- Training their staff on yet another tool

## Partnership models (ranked by feasibility)

### 1. Clinic-referral program (easiest, do first)

Local vets hand out printed cards with a unique referral code. Pet owner signs up, clinic gets a small kickback or free accounts, owner gets a free year or discount.

- Zero technical lift
- You don't touch the clinic's systems
- You're a recommendation, not a vendor
- Like a training school or grooming salon in the vet's referral network

**Target:** independent vet clinics first, not corporate chains. Concierge vets and boutique clinics are especially good fits because they value differentiation.

### 2. Co-branded "perk" offering (medium effort)

High-end clinics offer Homepage as a perk. *"We give all our new clients a free lifetime membership."*

- Clinic pays a bulk rate (maybe $10/client, one-time)
- Pet owner gets it free through the clinic
- Clinic markets it as part of their service
- You get distribution + bulk licensing revenue
- Positioning: this is the "Peloton for vets" play — aspirational brand lift for the clinic

### 3. Pre-visit summary integration (medium-high effort)

Clinics opt in to receive the auto-generated pre-visit summaries for upcoming appointments from their clients who use Homepage.

- Genuinely useful clinical workflow improvement
- Requires client opt-in for data sharing
- Vet sees a summary before the appointment: weight trends, symptoms logged, meds, questions the owner wants to ask
- Saves time and improves care
- Requires a vet-facing portal (additional product surface)

### 4. Specialty hospital partnerships (high-value niche)

Cancer centers, orthopedic specialists, cardiology clinics, dermatology.

- Their clients have chronic, complex pets with extensive records and multiple providers
- Homepage is dramatically more useful for these clients than for routine wellness
- Smaller market but higher intensity pain and higher willingness to pay
- Specialty clinics are also more tech-forward and willing to try new things

### 5. PIMS integration (hardest, far future)

Sync directly with Covetrus / IDEXX / Cornerstone / AVImark. Pet records populate automatically from the vet's own system.

- Holy grail because it removes all manual work
- Requires months-to-years of BD work with PIMS vendors
- PIMS vendors are notoriously slow and conservative
- Requires real company scale before they'll take a meeting
- This is a year 3+ play at earliest

## Why NOT to lead with vets

- **B2B sales cycles for vet practices are slow** — months per clinic
- **You need product validation first** — no vet will stake their reputation on an unproven tool
- **PetDesk already owns the shelf** — you can't dislodge them on their strength
- **Clinical decision-makers are scattered** — vet, office manager, tech lead, practice owner
- **The PMF signal doesn't come from vets** — it comes from pet owners loving the product

## When to start approaching vets

- **Year 1 (months 1-12):** consumer-only; reach 1,000-2,000 paying users; no vet work
- **Year 1.5:** start with 3-5 local clinics for referral program pilot; learn the sales motion
- **Year 2:** expand referral program; pursue specialty hospital partnerships
- **Year 2.5-3:** formalize co-branded offering; explore PIMS conversations

## Meta-point

Vet partnerships are a multiplier on an already-working product. They don't create product-market-fit; they amplify it. If the consumer product isn't loved on its own, partnerships won't save it. If it is loved, vets will start recommending it organically and partnerships formalize what's already happening.
